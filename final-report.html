ROOK QUEST 4.2 · The Quiet Responder

The game is an illustration, not Rook's real ranking. The numbers on the clear and warp screens come from the callout history, and the wording is proposed, not live. We do not know yet if the phone can show a message.

The rule change, in numbers

Today history.py takes 0.12 off a ranking score for a "no" and for a miss, adds only 0.08 for a yes, and never forgets. Proposal: a miss costs half as much (0.06), and scores drift back to neutral.

What happened

A step on release day, not a slow slide

Weekly acceptance sat at 75–78% for six weeks, dropped to 54% in the week 4.2 shipped, and has crept back to 72.7%. The "mostly seasonal" read from the handoff does not fit a break on 12 Aug.

Why it happened

The timeout started it. The scoring rule made it stick.

What the data shows is solid. The cause is a working hypothesis from reading the routing code, and I have labeled each link.

What the data rules out

The four are not pinged late in the chain (71% of their pings are first position, like everyone else). It is not a night-hours effect (50–77% missed at every hour). The ranking-weight change alone should favor home responders, so it is probably not the cause.

What I cannot say yet

Why the four miss (response time, device or notification change?). Per-responder ranking scores. Why the timeout was cut. Last August's numbers. The code is the repo's version, which may differ from production. My confidence: about 60 out of 95. What happened is solid; why is not.

How I found it

Six modules, six prompts that moved the work

For each module: one real finding and the exact prompt I wrote that produced it (typos kept).

MODULE 1

Origin Story

Get oriented. Read everything, frame the problems.
Finding

The break is a step on 12 Aug, not seasonal. Nine problems framed as user needs; 4.2 deliberately addressed only two of them. A ninth, found only in the data, was in no ticket: callouts nobody takes doubled from 5.5% to 11.1%.

My prompt (#13)compare problems against the callout data

Short, but it forced every ticket-and-interview problem to meet the numbers.

MODULE 2

Super Hearing

Listen to interviews and tickets, then compare.
Finding

Interviews and tickets agree on vanishing and quiet, but tickets show "gone quiet" outnumbers "gone before I could answer" 2 to 1. Interviews over-weighted dark mode, text size and the "callout is live" signal, which are old requests. Tickets: 40 before 12 Aug, 107 after.

My prompt (#16)You've now read both piles. Where do they disagree? What's loud in the interviews but rare in the tickets, and what's all over the tickets that nobody brought up in the interviews?
MODULE 3

Rewind

Rebuild the history from the database.
Finding

Daily acceptance was 68–75% on 10–11 Aug, then 40–53% on 12–16 Aug, with 4–12 missed pings a day versus 0–1 before. Splitting by responder showed four people hidden inside an average, and a Sankey chart showed outsiders taking most callouts in three areas.

My prompt (#3)why did you grouped some days like 10-11 august and 12-16 august?

Questioning the grouping led straight to the one-row-per-day table that exposed the cliff.

MODULE 4

X-Ray Vision

Read the routing code in plain English.
Finding

In history.py, only a yes adds points (+0.08); a decline and a miss both cost 0.12; nothing decays. A responder needs about 60% yes just to hold steady. A missed ping is treated as a choice, and it is not one.

My prompt (#10)Is there a way in the code to get more pings? because clearly the main problem is they are not getting enough pings to say yes so it's a vicious cycle

I named the loop before the code did. The answer: no lever exists, which is why the proposal changes the rule.

Keeping it from happening again

4.2 shipped with no owner, no success measure, no stated reason

The handoff said Priya "made calls faster than she checked them". The wiki's 4.2 roadmap entries are one line each, nothing explains the timeout cut, and the PM could not tell if 4.2 worked. The review-checklist skill makes those four gaps visible before a brief goes anywhere.

1 · Owner
Names who owns it and the decision to ship, stop or change.
4.2 gap: nobody on record for the timeout cut
2 · Success measure
A measure with baseline, target and a date, plus a stop condition.
4.2 gap: no success measure at all
3 · Same scope
What the opening promises is what the end measures.
4.2 gap: three changes shipped together
4 · Problem before fix
Who has the problem and the evidence, then the solution.
4.2 gap: fix described, reason missing
