# 02 · Super Hearing — prompts

**Context:** You joined Rook two weeks ago as PM on Dispatch.
Release 4.2 shipped on 12 August, just before you arrived, and
landed badly. Last session you built a context file and met the
company.

You still do not know what actually went wrong.

At the end of the session, type wrap up, and Claude Code saves the
prompts you wrote yourself below — not the starter prompt — updates
CLAUDE.md, and saves your work to GitHub. By Module 6 this file is a
prompt library built from your own questions.

---

### 1.

Read the four customer interviews in the Research section of Rook's wiki. These are four conversations with the people who run the console for our responders.

### 2.

Tell me what they're unhappy about, group it, tell me how many of the four raised each thing, and quote one line for each so I can hear how they actually said it.

### 3.

Now I would like to understand how much these unhappy comments matter in terms of how much they impact business. Use the same 6 groups you just gave me, rank them in terms of severity, urgency and impact on the business.

### 4.

Let's take a step back and align on what I need from you. In order to better understand how we even arrived at 4.2, I want to better understand the research from customer interviews. Before you share anything, interview me so that you understand what I require as a Product Manager at Rook Industries responsible for all things Dispatch. Ask me one question at a time.

### 5.

a call about what to do next on 4.2

### 6.

when I say a call about what to do next on 4.2 I meant I need to make a decision on what to do next on 4.2 (change things, improve, keep, ect). Not a call

### 7.

I would like to understand what happened before 4.2, what was done, what we were trying to achieve with this new release, what was actually achieved, what problems rose that are impacting the business but also the silent problems. The gap is that there is a lot of information I don't know about that I need to understand

### 8.

### 9.

### 10.

### 11.

Based on the interviews, the trouble with 4.2 is? In one sentence

### 12.

Let's bring in the tickets data next

### 13.

Same treatment as before: group them, tell me how many are in each group, and quote one line from each.

### 14.

put the two reads side by side: interviews and tickets reads

### 15.

With all this information, do the priorities change in terms of what needs attention more urgent? Did we find something new we did not know when we only read the interviews?

### 16.

You've now read both piles. Where do they disagree? What's loud in the interviews but rare in the tickets, and what's all over the tickets that nobody brought up in the interviews?

### 17.

After all of this, does this sentence still holds: Based on the interviews, 4.2 left some responders losing callouts before they can answer while others go quiet for weeks, and handlers can neither see nor explain why.?

### 18.

If I'd only read the interviews, what would I have got wrong?
